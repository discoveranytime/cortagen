Cortagen
the cortex peptide

A 4-amino-acid peptide (Ala-Glu-Asp-Pro) from the Khavinson bioregulator family, aimed at the brain's cortex — the wrinkled outer layer where thinking happens. Claimed to support cortical neurons and nervous-system recovery; published evidence is minimal. Here is what it actually is, separated from the hype.

Quick read

Cortagen (AEDP) is a synthetic tetrapeptide bioregulator developed in St. Petersburg, described as cortex-specific: in bioregulator theory it tunes gene expression in cortical tissue. Russian sources credit it with neuroprotective effects and support for nervous-system function, largely from animal work. But independent research is scarce, there are no Western trials, and no regulator has approved it. Scroll for the full picture.

Four amino acids aimed at the most human part of the brain. Cortagen (Ala-Glu-Asp-Pro) is Pinealon's close cousin — one amino acid longer, pointed at the cortex instead of the brain in general.

02The cortex angle

Why the cortex?

The cortex is where strokes, trauma, and aging do their most visible damage. Cortagen's origin story is the bioregulator program pointing its shortest tools at the brain's most complex tissue.

Aiming the shortest tools at the most complex tissue.
The cortex bet

The cortex holds most of what we call thinking — and it's fragile: stroke, injury, and age hit it first. A cortex-tuning peptide is the bioregulator program's most ambitious brain bet.

AEDP vs AEDG

One amino acid separates Cortagen (AEDP) from Epitalon (AEDG) — yet they're claimed for different organs. In the bioregulator view, tiny sequence changes redirect tissue specificity.

The verification gap

Like all bioregulators, Cortagen's story is told almost entirely by its own school — with little outside measurement.

10Understanding Cortagen Terminology

Understanding Cortagen Terminology

Eleven terms, each with the science and a plain-English translation. Tap any term to expand it.

The scienceThe cerebral cortex is the thin, folded outer layer of the cerebrum, composed of layered gray matter densely packed with neurons and their connections. It carries out most higher-order functions: sensory perception, voluntary movement, language, memory, and conscious thought. It is also among the most metabolically demanding and injury-sensitive tissues in the body. This infographic positions the cortex as Cortagen’s claimed target tissue, while treating all brain-directed effects as proposed rather than demonstrated.

Plain EnglishThe cerebral cortex is the brain’s thin, wrinkly outer surface — the layer that does your thinking, planning, talking, and remembering. It is also the brain tissue that takes the biggest beating in a head injury and is the hardest to repair. This infographic talks about it as the place Cortagen is supposed to work on. But remember the framing: everything about Cortagen helping this tissue is proposed, not proven.

The scienceA tetrapeptide is a peptide molecule made of exactly four amino acid residues linked by peptide bonds. As a very short peptide it is far simpler to synthesize than a protein, and its small size means it can, in principle, move through biological compartments that larger molecules cannot. Cortagen is described in this infographic as a tetrapeptide — a four-amino-acid molecule in the Khavinson-style short-peptide tradition. Its small size alone, however, proves nothing about whether it reaches any particular tissue in the body.

Plain EnglishA tetrapeptide is just a tiny protein fragment made of four building blocks called amino acids, linked together like four beads on a string. Because it is so small, it is cheap to make and easy to study. Cortagen is described as one of these four-block peptides. But being small does not mean it actually gets into the brain — that part is still unproven.

The scienceIn the Khavinson school of research, a bioregulator is a short synthetic peptide — typically two to four amino acids long — proposed to regulate the activity of a specific organ or tissue, often through effects on gene expression. The concept holds that different peptide sequences correspond to different target tissues, with each compound framed as organ-specific. This is the research tradition Cortagen belongs to. This infographic presents the bioregulatory mechanism strictly as proposed, not demonstrated.

Plain EnglishIn the research tradition Cortagen comes from, a “bioregulator” is a tiny man-made peptide that is supposed to tune up one specific organ — like a remote control programmed for just one device. Each short peptide is claimed to match a different organ. That is the framework behind all of Cortagen’s claims. But the key word is “supposed to”: the tuning effect is proposed, not shown.

The scienceGene expression is the process by which the information encoded in a gene is converted into a functional product, most often a protein, determining which genes are active in a cell at a given time. It is controlled through transcription and downstream regulation, and it defines a cell’s behavior and identity. Changes in gene expression are how tissues respond to stress, damage, and recovery. Cortagen’s proposed mechanism, as framed in this infographic, involves tuning gene expression in neural tissue — a claim presented as proposed, not demonstrated.

Plain EnglishYour genes are like a huge library of recipes, and “gene expression” just means which recipes a cell is actually cooking up right now. Those choices decide what the cell does — whether it grows, repairs, rests, or dies. Different cells read different recipes, which is why a brain cell and a skin cell behave so differently. The infographic presents Cortagen’s proposed mechanism as adjusting which recipes nerve cells are cooking — but that is a proposal, not a proven fact.

The scienceA neuron is an excitable cell specialized for receiving, conducting, and transmitting electrochemical signals; it is the fundamental signaling unit of the nervous system. A typical neuron consists of a cell body, branching dendrites that receive inputs, and an axon that carries output signals. In the central nervous system, neurons lost to injury or disease are generally not replaced, since adult neurogenesis is extremely limited. This infographic frames Cortagen’s proposed action as protecting cortical neurons, and keeps that brain claim distinct from any peripheral-nerve narrative.

Plain EnglishA neuron is a nerve cell — the brain and nervous system’s basic messenger. It has branches that pick up incoming signals and a long wire that sends outgoing ones. Unlike skin or liver cells, brain neurons lost to injury generally do not grow back. This infographic frames Cortagen as being about defending these brain cells specifically — separate from the peripheral-nerve story it also discusses.

The scienceNeuroprotection refers to strategies that preserve the structure and function of neurons against injury, toxicity, or degenerative processes. It may involve reducing cell death, limiting oxidative stress, or supporting cellular repair pathways. Demonstrated neuroprotection requires controlled evidence that a treatment actually reduces neuronal loss or improves neurological outcomes. In this infographic, neuroprotection is the core claim made for Cortagen, and it is explicitly framed as the least-measured, most speculative part of the story.

Plain EnglishNeuroprotection just means keeping nerve cells alive and working when something threatens them — like an injury or a disease. Proving it means actually showing that fewer cells died, in a proper experiment. For Cortagen, “protecting brain cells” is the headline claim. And this infographic is honest about the gap: it is the part of the story with the least actual evidence behind it.

The scienceA concussion is a mild traumatic brain injury caused by a blow, jolt, or rapid acceleration and deceleration of the head, temporarily disrupting normal brain function. Symptoms can include headache, confusion, dizziness, and cognitive fog, and most concussions resolve within weeks without any specific treatment. It is the most common acute injury to cortical tissue and the scenario behind many of the online anecdotes discussed in this infographic. Nothing here presents Cortagen as an established treatment for concussion or any form of TBI.

Plain EnglishA concussion is what happens when your brain gets rattled inside your skull — from a hit, a fall, or a sudden stop. It can cause headaches, confusion, dizziness, and that foggy feeling, though most concussions get better on their own within weeks. It is the most common way the brain’s outer layer gets injured. It is also the situation behind most of the Cortagen stories floating around online. Nothing here presents Cortagen as an actual treatment for it.

The scienceAn antioxidant is a molecule that neutralizes reactive oxygen species — free radicals — preventing or limiting the oxidative damage these unstable molecules inflict on cells. Oxidative stress occurs when free-radical production exceeds the body’s own antioxidant defenses, and it contributes to cell injury in many disease and injury states. Some protective effects attributed to peptides are proposed to work through antioxidant action. In this infographic, an antioxidant effect is part of Cortagen’s proposed mechanism — not a demonstrated outcome.

Plain EnglishAn antioxidant is a molecule that soaks up “free radicals” — unstable particles that chew up cells the way rust chews up metal. When these particles outnumber the body’s own cleanup crew, that is called oxidative stress, and it can damage cells. Some of the protective effects claimed for peptides are supposed to work this way. For Cortagen, an antioxidant effect is part of the proposed mechanism — it has not been demonstrated.

The scienceThe blood-brain barrier is a selective permeability barrier formed by the tightly joined endothelial cells of cerebral capillaries, reinforced by astrocyte processes and pericytes. It restricts the passage of most molecules, ions, and pathogens from the bloodstream into brain tissue while allowing essential nutrients through. This barrier is a major obstacle for any compound intended to act on the brain. This infographic notes that Cortagen’s ability to cross it — a precondition for its claimed cortical effects — is unproven.

Plain EnglishThe blood-brain barrier is the brain’s security checkpoint: the walls of the brain’s blood vessels are sealed extra-tight so most things in the blood cannot just wander into brain tissue. Only selected nutrients and gases get waved through. It is a big reason why many medicines that work elsewhere fail in the brain. This infographic points out that Cortagen has not been shown to get through this checkpoint — and its claimed brain effects depend on it doing so.

The scienceA placebo effect is an improvement in a patient’s condition that results from the expectation of treatment rather than from the treatment’s pharmacological action. Placebo responses are strongest for subjective, self-reported outcomes such as pain, fatigue, and cognitive fog. They cannot be separated from true drug effects without randomized, controlled comparison. This infographic raises the placebo explanation explicitly, because the subjective recoveries described in online Cortagen anecdotes are exactly the kind of outcomes where placebo responses are largest.

Plain EnglishThe placebo effect is when you feel better simply because you expect a treatment to work — not because the substance did anything. It is strongest for things only you can report: pain, tiredness, brain fog. The only way to tell it apart from a real drug effect is a proper controlled trial. This infographic brings it up on purpose: the “fog lifted” stories around Cortagen describe exactly the kind of symptoms where placebo effects are biggest.

The scienceA research chemical is a substance sold for laboratory research purposes rather than as an approved medicine, without the standardized manufacturing, quality oversight, and clinical safety testing that approved drugs undergo. Many short peptides circulate in this category despite lacking approved indications. Products sold this way carry unknown purity and unverified safety profiles. This infographic places Cortagen in this category while the top and bottom disclaimers state the overall terms of the content.

Plain EnglishA “research chemical” is a substance sold as lab material rather than as a proper medicine. Products in this category do not go through the manufacturing standards, quality checks, and safety testing that real drugs must pass. A lot of short peptides, including Cortagen-style ones, are sold this way. So there is no guarantee of what is actually in the vial or what it does.
